❯ the problem

Watching tutorials isn't learning to code.

A video makes every line look obvious. Then you open an empty editor and nothing comes. Following along trains you to recognize code; writing it needs recall, feedback on your own mistakes, and practice spread out over weeks. CodeCoach is built around those three.

feels like learning
  • Watching someone else type
  • Re-reading notes and slides
  • Copying a solution that works
  • Cramming a topic in one night
❯ what actually sticks
  • Predicting what code does before running it
  • Writing it yourself, with less help each time
  • Tests that tell you exactly what's wrong
  • Coming back to it right before you'd forget

❯ how it works

Learn it. Climb it. Remember it.

Learn

The coach explains one idea with a worked example, then asks you to predict what a snippet prints before you run it. Short explanations, your words back, no wall of text.

Climb

Each topic is a ladder from typing one line from memory to timed problems from scratch. Support fades as you go up. Mastered means three new problems solved without hints.

Remember

Solved topics come back on a schedule, mixed with other topics so you practice choosing the right tool. Struggle and the next review moves closer.

❯ who it's for

For anyone who wants to write code, not just read it.

Students in a class

Drop in your lecture notes, slides and practice quizzes. The coach follows your class's style and format, drills what your quizzes test, and respects its AI policy: hints only on graded work.

Self-learners

Start a track for Python, Java, C++, C, JavaScript, Go or Rust. The coach builds a roadmap from where you are and keeps your progress in notes you can read.

Blank-editor freezers

If you understand the lecture but can't start the code, you begin lower on the ladder: put lines in order, fill in gaps, fix a bug. The help fades as your recall grows.

❯ your data

Your data stays yours.

All your notes, solutions and progress live in a plain folder on your computer. Read or edit any of it in any text editor, or open the folder as an Obsidian vault. Sync it with the tools you already use. There is no CodeCoach account and no CodeCoach cloud.

   └─ Learner Profile.md    how you learn best
  • Plain Markdown. TextEdit, Notepad, VS Code and Obsidian all open it. If CodeCoach disappeared tomorrow, your work would still be there.
  • Sync for free. Put the folder in iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or Google Drive, or track it with Git or Syncthing. Point CodeCoach on your other computer at the same folder.
  • Keys stay local. AI keys live in ~/.codecoach/config.json on each computer. They never go into your Library or an export.
  • No tracking. CodeCoach talks only to the AI you choose.
Export everything In the app: Welcome → Export everything. One zip with every course, solution, session and setting.

Which languages?
Coding problems with tests in Java, Python, C++, C and JavaScript. The Playground also runs Go and Rust. Any language works for explanations and quizzes.

        
Do I need an account?
No. Pick a folder, pick an AI, start studying.

        
Can I use it for graded work?
Each course stores its AI policy. When a class forbids AI-written code, the coach gives hints and explanations only, never solutions to real assignments.

        
How do I move to a new computer?
Sync or copy your Library folder, install CodeCoach, and choose that folder. Or use Export everything and unzip it on the new machine.
